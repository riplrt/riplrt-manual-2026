7  Roles, Responsibilities, and Project Pod Structure

{#sec-roles-pods}

This chapter defines who does what. It preserves the role clarity of the original manual and adds the project pod model — the structure that lets a large, hybrid portfolio run without overloading any one person.

7.1 General expectations for every member

Regardless of role, every RIPLRT member is expected to:

  • Maintain curiosity and care for the science and projects they’re involved in.
  • Be efficient without rushing — plan before doing, and troubleshoot ahead of time.
  • Learn from mistakes and own them quickly. Mistakes are correctable; hiding them is not.
  • Ask for help — pride is fine, too much pride is costly.
  • Keep up with science relevant to our mission.
  • Practice time management and meet commitments, communicating early when they can’t.
  • Maintain a respectful, professional atmosphere and use emotional intelligence in conflict.
  • Work honestly: never plagiarize, fabricate, or falsify. A negative result is still a result. (Re-analyzing publicly available datasets is not plagiarism — it is good open science.)
  • Keep availability current so work can be planned realistically.

7.2 Standing roles

7.2.1 Principal Investigator (PI)

Dr. Félix E. Rivera-Mariani sets scientific direction and models the group’s values. The PI:

  • Is the foundation and example for the group.
  • Mentors members across science, learning, professional, and career dimensions.
  • Provides timely feedback across all scientific aspects.
  • Finds and applies for funding and identifies collaborations.
  • Has members’ backs when they’ve followed the manual, and strives to be impartial.
  • Chairs lab meetings unless delegated.
  • Holds final accountability for integrity, authorship, and data governance.

7.2.2 Group Lead / Lab Coordinator

Assists the PI in running the group: coordinating meetings and agendas, overseeing onboarding/offboarding, maintaining shared infrastructure, helping route tasks, and mentoring members. (This role generalizes the former “Lab Manager,” reframed for a hybrid group where the work is data and projects more than benches and reagents.)

7.2.3 Research Assistants / Senior Mentees

Carry out core analysis and writing, mentor more junior members, generate research questions, and help maintain reproducibility standards. Often serve as project leads (below).

7.2.4 Graduate Students

Drive projects, learn methods quickly, mentor undergraduates, generate questions, and pursue professional development they identify themselves as well as opportunities the PI surfaces.

7.2.5 Undergraduate Interns / Trainees

Learn skills and procedures, contribute to defined project tasks, and pursue professional development. Every trainee is mentored and mapped to a real deliverable.

7.2.6 Mentors and Mentees

Mentoring is woven through every role (?sec-mentoring). Mentors are the first line of support for ensuring mentees understand and follow this manual.

NoteFlexible, not punitive

This manual replaces the old wage-tier role mechanics (waged/non-waged status changes tied to task completion or precision thresholds) with professional accountability handled through documentation, feedback, and — where applicable — institutional or contractual performance processes (?sec-accountability). Compensation and employment terms are governed by the relevant institution or entity, not by this manual.

7.3 The project pod model

A portfolio this size cannot run on one-on-one PI mentoring alone. We organize work into pods: small, project-specific teams with defined roles. Pods distribute leadership, build mentee independence, and protect the PI’s strategic bandwidth — this is training leadership, not distancing from students.

Each pod is chartered using the Project Pod Charter template (/templates/project-pod-charter.qmd) before work begins.

7.3.1 Pod roles

A typical pod assigns these roles (one person may hold more than one; junior members co-hold roles to learn them):

Role Owns
PI Scientific direction, funding, final integrity/authorship accountability
Project Lead Day-to-day coordination, timeline, the pod charter, weekly task structure
Data Steward Data access, data-use agreement compliance, variable dictionary, secure storage
Analysis Lead Statistical/ML analysis plan, code, reproducibility of results
Writing Lead Manuscript/abstract drafting and submission coordination
Visualization / Dashboard Lead Figures, dashboards, decision-support prototypes
Reproducibility Lead Repo structure, environment, analysis checklist, render integrity
Science Communication Lead Community briefs, bilingual materials, social/science-comm content
Trainee Contributors Defined, mentored tasks that ladder up to a named deliverable

7.3.2 Tiered mentoring within pods

Pods use a senior mentee / project lead → junior mentees → PI oversight structure. The project lead supports junior members day to day; the PI provides scientific oversight and steps in on key decisions (design, authorship, submission). This tiered structure is how we scale mentoring sustainably.

7.3.3 One rule that keeps us healthy

ImportantEvery new member maps to a real deliverable

The group’s risk is not too few people — it is overextension. Every new mentee must map to a funded or near-funded project, a specific deliverable, and a defined supervision pathway before they join a pod. Growth is intentional, aligned with mentoring bandwidth, not maximal.

7.4 Project operating manuals

Each major project (e.g., PR respiratory risk, MESA, AsthmaNet, Framingham/ARIC, All of Us, biomarker projects) maintains a short project operating manual — effectively the completed pod charter plus a two-page operational summary: aims, datasets, access rules, variable dictionary, deliverables, authorship plan, and weekly task structure. These reduce cognitive load and let mentees work more independently.
