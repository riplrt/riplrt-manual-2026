# RIPLRT Institute Code of Conduct

*2026 Edition (v2.0)*

The RIPLRT Institute is committed to providing a safe, welcoming, inclusive, and harassment-free environment for everyone — students, staff, collaborators, community partners, and visitors — regardless of age, race, ethnicity, national origin, immigration status, language, disability, neurotype, religion, sex, gender identity or expression, sexual orientation, socioeconomic background, caregiving status, veteran status, or career stage.

This Code applies to all RIPLRT spaces and activities: in-person and virtual meetings, fieldwork and community engagement, shared digital platforms (code repositories, project workspaces, document storage, messaging, email), conferences and travel undertaken on behalf of the group, and interactions with collaborators and the public when representing RIPLRT.

This Code complements — and does not replace — Lynn University policies, IRB requirements, sponsor terms, and applicable law. Where those impose stricter or additional obligations, they govern.

## Our values in practice

We translate our core values into everyday behavior. We expect every member to:

- **Be respectful and inclusive.** Treat everyone with dignity. Make space for quieter voices. Assume good faith.
- **Support psychological safety.** Questions, "I don't know," and honest mistakes are part of learning. We normalize learning in public, especially for those new to coding, statistics, or environmental-health methods.
- **Give and receive feedback constructively.** Critique ideas and data, not people. Deliver feedback with the same care and rigor you bring to your science.
- **Act with integrity.** No fabrication, falsification, or plagiarism. Honor authorship and contributorship agreements. Represent data and uncertainty honestly.
- **Protect people and data.** Follow IRB, data-governance, privacy, and de-identification rules. Never share identifiable participant information or restricted data.
- **Communicate with "no surprises."** Raise problems early. Renegotiate deadlines responsibly rather than going silent.
- **Honor consent and privacy among members.** Do not photograph, record, or share images of members without their consent, and never of human participants. Get PI approval before posting group-related content publicly.

## Unacceptable behavior

The following are not tolerated:

- Harassment, intimidation, bullying, or discrimination in any form.
- Sexual harassment, unwanted sexual attention, or inappropriate physical contact.
- Demeaning, derogatory, or exclusionary comments, including microaggressions, directed at any individual or group.
- Retaliation against anyone who raises a concern or reports a violation in good faith.
- Research misconduct (fabrication, falsification, plagiarism) or violations of data-governance and IRB rules.
- Sharing identifiable participant data or restricted datasets, or violating a data-use agreement.
- Sustained disruption of meetings, deliberate undermining of colleagues, or abuse of mentoring authority.

## Mentoring and power dynamics

Mentors, project leads, and the PI hold positions of influence. With that comes added responsibility: to model the behavior we expect, to avoid using authority in ways that pressure or exploit, to give credit fairly, and to make accountability about *improvement and documentation*, never humiliation. The greater the authority, the greater the obligation to uphold this Code.

Reports are taken seriously and handled with as much confidentiality as possible. We recognize that some matters must be escalated to institutional authorities, and that certain roles carry mandatory-reporting obligations; we will be honest about those limits rather than promise absolute confidentiality.

We do not tolerate retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith.

## How concerns are handled

When a concern is raised, we aim to:

1. **Listen** to the reporting person and take their experience seriously.
2. **Clarify** what happened, hearing all parties fairly.
3. **Act** proportionately — ranging from a facilitated conversation, to a documented expectation/performance-improvement step, to escalation to institutional authorities for serious violations.
4. **Follow up** to confirm the behavior has changed and the reporting person is supported.

Serious violations (harassment, discrimination, research misconduct, data breaches) may result in removal from the group and referral to the appropriate Lynn University or sponsor authorities, consistent with institutional policy and law.

## A shared commitment

This Code works only if we all uphold it — and it protects all of us. By being part of RIPLRT, we each commit to creating the kind of scientific home we would want for ourselves and for those who come after us.
